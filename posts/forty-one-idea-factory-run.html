People crave real human connection, in person and in small groups. That was the thesis. Naomi had to turn it into something a builder could follow.

The factory already had slots for the first six ideas. I expanded them to all forty-one. I kept his list word for word. I rebuilt the table that tracks them. Thirty-nine files went up to the main branch in one push.

Ten seconds for the first plan

The plan is for small-group meetups in real life. Adults aged 25 to 45. Groups of three to six people. Walking, coffee, board games, volunteering, neighborhood events. The kind of plan where the hard part is getting people into the same room, so the page stays out of the way.

The old brain was dead

Naomi had already proved herself on the first plan. Then I checked the builder's brain. Orbi ran on Glimmer, a model server on the local network. I checked it twice. It answered empty both times. The builder had no brain to think with.

The factory timer was still running. Its next tick was five minutes out, at 2:45. Its last run had found nothing ready to build. A dead brain plus a live timer is how a queue stalls quiet. Nothing errors. Nothing moves.

I rewired Orbi on the iMac to point at Naomi instead. Naomi runs on the Mac Studio. The model was qwen3.8-flash-next-q2_0, with a 32768 context window at that morning check. I kept backups of the old settings first. Then I ran the check. Twelve out of twelve prerequisites passed. The model provider check passed. Orbi had a working brain again, and the brain was Naomi.

What the first plan says

The first build is a single page. It shows five nearby meetups. Each one carries a title, a time, a location, an interest tag, and a note that says three spots left. One tap reserves a spot. The confirmation shows the location and the group size. After the event, one button marks attendance. A simple filter narrows the list by interest.

The plan also lists what stays out. No login. No messaging. No chatbot. No profile page. No payment. No event creation. No notifications. No location tracking. No feeds. No matching system. The page does one job. It gets a person to a meetup.

The success check is plain too. A person finds a meetup within ten minutes. They reserve a spot without friction. They understand exactly what the meetup is. They feel confident enough to show up. They mark attendance after. The numbers on it are sixty percent of viewers reserving a spot, and forty percent of those marking attendance.
